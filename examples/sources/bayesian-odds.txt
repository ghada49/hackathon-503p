Project-authored teaching note. A binary hypothesis H has prior probability p with 0 < p < 1. Its prior odds are p/(1-p). Evidence E contributes likelihood ratio L = P(E|H)/P(E|not H), where L is positive. Posterior odds are L times prior odds. Converting odds o back to probability gives o/(1+o). The resulting posterior probability is (L*p)/(1-p+L*p). Larger L increases belief in H; L=1 leaves the prior unchanged. This is one evidence update, not a general model of dependent evidence. Use prior probability and likelihood ratio as two independent controls. Keep p away from 0 and 1 and L strictly positive. Posterior remains between 0 and 1. Suggested explorations: neutral evidence L=1, and evidence favoring H with L>1.
